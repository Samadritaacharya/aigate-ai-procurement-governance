# Market context — Germany / EU, September 2026

AIGate is designed around a concrete operational gap: companies are adopting AI faster than many can govern, procure, evidence, and scale it.

## Signals behind the product

- **German SME adoption is real but uneven.** KfW reported in February 2026 that 20% of German SMEs use AI, with adoption higher among larger and more innovative firms. KfW's July 2026 analysis also noted that simple, broadly applicable AI uses dominate while advanced data analytics remains much less common.
- **Procurement is being asked to scale AI responsibly.** BME's July 2026 AI-procurement forum explicitly framed the challenge as moving from pilots to responsible scaling, including data quality, roles, compliance, risk management, regulation, liability, governance, and long-term operations.
- **Digital supplier/procurement maturity remains uneven.** BME reported in June 2026 that only 18.6% of surveyed procurement leaders considered automation highly mature and only 6.0% considered AI use highly mature.
- **The AI Act is now an operating constraint, not a distant concept.** The European Commission states that the AI Act became broadly applicable on 2 August 2026, while some high-risk obligations have later application dates. Transparency duties under Article 50 apply from 2 August 2026.

## Product response

AIGate does not attempt to replace lawyers, procurement specialists, security teams, or privacy professionals. It turns their coordination problem into an explicit workflow:

`use case → value → data → vendor → risk indicators → evidence → controls → approvals → monitoring`

The public implementation deliberately uses screening labels such as **high-risk candidate** and **prohibited-review indicator** rather than claiming a legally binding classification.
